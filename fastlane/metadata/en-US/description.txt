A READING COMPANION TO LISTEN TO YOURSELF DIFFERENTLY

An Other Perspective is an app for personal reading and reflection, inspired by the great traditions of human thought. Share a thought, a situation, an emotion — receive multiple perspectives drawn from philosophy, literature and the wisdom traditions of the world.

No judgement. No moralising advice. Just angles that broaden your view and bring movement back to your thinking.

— ONE THOUGHT, MANY PERSPECTIVES —

• Choose the sources that speak to you: philosophers (Aristotle, Spinoza, Hannah Arendt, Nietzsche, Sartre, Camus…), eastern wisdoms (Buddhism, Advaita Vedanta, Bhakti, Taoism, Zen…), spiritual traditions (Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Sufism, Hinduism…), contemporary authors and thinkers.

• Each perspective is written in the tone and with the concepts of the chosen source. You stay free to keep what nourishes you.

— A JOURNAL FOR YOUR JOURNEY —

• Your reflections and the perspectives you receive are kept in a personal journal.

• An emotion wheel and a timeline to recognise and name what runs through you.

• Alphabetical index of sources, smooth navigation, voice reading of texts.

— AMY, YOUR GUIDE —

A kind voice that orients you in the app and answers your questions, at your own pace.

— A RESPECTFUL APPROACH —

An Other Perspective contains no advertising, no tracker, and no behavioural analytics tool. Your data stays primarily on your device. See our privacy policy for details.

— IMPORTANT NOTICE —

An Other Perspective is a tool for cultural reading and personal reflection. It is not a medical device, not a therapeutic tool, and not a substitute for psychological care or medical advice. The perspectives offered are excerpts and reformulations of philosophical, literary and spiritual texts — they do not constitute therapeutic advice. The app was not developed in partnership with a licensed mental health professional.

If you are experiencing emotional distress, a crisis, or seeking guidance, please consult a qualified professional (doctor, psychologist, psychiatrist). In case of life-threatening emergency, contact your local emergency services (911 in the US, 112 in Europe) or a suicide prevention hotline.
